Five months into Matt's AI-assisted recompilation work, the important result was not a single game or console. It was a repeatable pattern: each additional title exposes assumptions in the framework, and after several varied games the next bring-up can become dramatically less bespoke.

The article looks across PlayStation, NES, SNES, Genesis, GBA, and other experiments to ask where AI genuinely accelerated unfamiliar systems work—and where verification, debugging, and human judgment still determined whether the result was trustworthy.

That history matters here because Retro Porting Toolkit is built around the reusable knowledge left behind by those iterations, not the novelty of any one generated patch.
